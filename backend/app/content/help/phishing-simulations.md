A phishing simulation sends a realistic but harmless test email to your staff. People who click see a short lesson on what they missed. Over time, click rates fall, and you have evidence for awareness controls.

## Safety by design

- Emails go **only to your own company's email domains** (your verified domains and your admins' domains). Personal addresses such as Gmail are skipped automatically.
- Emails are sent **from the platform's own address**, with a display name such as "IT Helpdesk". They never spoof your domain or a real brand.
- The landing page **collects nothing**: no passwords, no form fields. It records only that the link was opened.
- **Link scanners don't count as clicks.** Email security gateways open links automatically; a click is recorded only when the page loads in a real browser.
- Individual results are visible to **admins only**. Use them to coach, not to blame.

## Templates

| Template | Difficulty | Pretends to be |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Password expires today | Easy | IT Helpdesk |
| Missed delivery | Easy | Courier |
| Shared document | Medium | File sharing |
| Overdue invoice | Medium | Accounts receivable |
| UPI refund | Medium | Payments team |
| HR policy acknowledgement | Hard | HR team |
| Urgent request from leadership | Hard | Office of the CEO |

Each template comes with the warning signs people should have noticed, shown on the lesson page and in the results.

## Running a simulation

1. **Training → Phishing simulations → New simulation**.
2. Name it, pick a template (a preview is shown), and choose departments, or leave them all unticked to send to everyone.
3. Decide whether to **assign the phishing course to anyone who clicks** (on by default; due in 14 days).
4. **Save as draft**. The draft shows how many people it will go to.
5. Before launching, ask IT to **allow-list the platform's sending address** so the emails aren't filtered, and let leadership know a test is running.
6. **Launch**. Emails are sent in the background.
7. Watch results come in: delivered, clicked, click rate overall and by department, and each person's result.
8. **Close** the simulation when you're done; later clicks are no longer counted.

Sent simulations are kept as training evidence and can't be deleted, only closed.

## What people see when they click

A page titled **This was a phishing simulation**, explaining that nothing happened and no information was collected, the email they clicked, what should have made them pause, three habits for next time, and a link to the phishing course if they were enrolled.

## Good practice

- Run one every quarter, increasing the difficulty as click rates fall.
- Vary templates; people share what they saw.
- Celebrate reporting: people who report suspicious emails quickly are your best defence.
