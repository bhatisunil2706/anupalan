New to compliance? These are the words you'll see most often, in plain language.

## A to D

**Access review**: A periodic check that everyone with access to a system still needs it. See [People and access](/docs/people-and-access).

**Acceptance criteria**: What a piece of evidence must show for a control, listed on each control so uploaders and reviewers agree. See [Evidence review](/docs/evidence-review).

**Audit**: An independent check, by an auditor, that you do what your framework requires. A SOC 2 Type II or ISO 27001 certification audit looks at evidence across a period, not just one day.

**Audit log**: The tamper-evident record of every action in the platform. See [Organisation settings](/docs/organisation-settings#audit-log).

**Auditor sampling**: Choosing a random selection of evidence to test instead of everything, as auditors do. The selection is reproducible. See [Audit workspace](/docs/audit-workspace#evidence-sampling).

**CERT-In**: India's national computer emergency response team. Its 2022 directions require reporting certain cyber incidents within **6 hours**. See [Incidents](/docs/incidents).

**Consent Manager**: A company registered with the Data Protection Board that lets people give, manage and withdraw consent across many businesses.

**Control**: Something your company does to manage a risk, such as "MFA is required for all staff". One control can satisfy requirements in several frameworks. See [Key concepts](/docs/concepts#controls).

**DAKSH**: The RBI's supervisory portal, where RBI-regulated entities report cyber incidents within 6 hours.

**Data Fiduciary**: Under the DPDP Act, the business that decides why and how personal data is processed: usually you.

**Data Principal**: The person the personal data is about: your customer, user or employee. For a child, the parent or guardian acts for them.

**Data Processor**: A company that processes personal data on your behalf, such as a cloud provider or a payroll service. Track them as [vendors](/docs/vendors).

**Data Protection Board (DPB)**: The regulator that enforces the DPDP Act and receives breach reports.

**DPDP Act**: India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, and the DPDP Rules, 2025. See [DPDP at a glance](/docs/dpdp-overview).

**DPO**: Data Protection Officer. Required for Significant Data Fiduciaries; others must still name a contact for privacy questions.

## E to P

**Evidence**: Proof that a control works: a document, screenshot, export, link or an automated check result. See [Evidence](/docs/evidence).

**Evidence pack**: A single download of everything an auditor needs, with fingerprints for each file. See [Audit workspace](/docs/audit-workspace#evidence-pack).

**Fingerprint (SHA-256 hash)**: A unique code calculated from a file's contents. If even one byte changes, the fingerprint changes, which proves a file hasn't been altered.

**Framework**: A published set of requirements, such as ISO 27001, SOC 2, DPDP, GDPR or PCI DSS. See [Frameworks](/docs/frameworks).

**Gap**: A requirement that no working control covers yet.

**Incident**: Something that has, or might have, harmed the security of your systems or data. A **personal data breach** is an incident affecting personal data.

**Inherent and residual risk**: How serious a risk is before your controls (inherent) and after them (residual). See [Risk register](/docs/risk-register).

**ISMS**: Information Security Management System: the policies, processes and people ISO 27001 asks you to run. This platform is where yours lives.

**LSP (lending service provider)**: A company that does lending work (sourcing, app, collections) for a bank or NBFC under the RBI Digital Lending Directions.

**MFA / two-step verification**: Signing in with something you know (a password) and something you have (a phone app code).

**NBFC layers**: The RBI groups NBFCs into Base, Middle, Upper and Top Layers by size and risk. The 2026 Cybersecurity Directions apply lighter requirements to smaller Base Layer NBFCs.

**Offboarding**: Removing a leaver's access to every system, quickly and provably.

**Payment aggregator (PA)**: A company that collects payments for merchants, online or in person, authorised by the RBI. Payment gateways only provide the technology.

**Payment system operator (PSO)**: A company authorised by the RBI to run a payment system, such as a wallet (PPI) issuer, card network or ATM network.

**Phishing**: Fake messages that trick people into clicking a link or giving away a password. See [Phishing simulations](/docs/phishing-simulations).

**Policy**: A short written rule your company agrees to follow, approved by management and read by staff. See [Policies](/docs/policies).

**Processing activity**: One reason you use personal data, such as "payroll" or "marketing emails", recorded in the [data inventory](/docs/data-inventory) (also called a record of processing activities, or RoPA).

## R to Z

**Readiness**: The share of a framework's requirements covered by healthy controls. See [Key concepts](/docs/concepts#how-readiness-is-calculated).

**Rights request**: A person asking to see, correct, erase or otherwise control their data. See [Rights requests](/docs/rights-requests).

**Risk register**: Your list of what could go wrong, how likely and how bad it is, and what you're doing about it.

**RLS (row-level security)**: The database feature that keeps each company's data separate. See [How we protect your data](/docs/platform-security).

**Scope**: What an audit or framework covers: which systems, teams, locations and data.

**Significant Data Fiduciary (SDF)**: A business the government designates because of the volume or sensitivity of its data. SDFs have extra DPDP duties such as a DPO, data protection impact assessments and audits.

**SOC 2**: A US-origin attestation that many customers ask SaaS companies for. **Type I** checks the design of controls on one date; **Type II** checks they worked over a period (usually 3 to 12 months).

**SSO (single sign-on)**: Signing in with your company's Google, Microsoft or Okta account instead of a separate password.

**Statement of Applicability (SoA)**: ISO 27001's list of Annex A controls, with whether each applies and why. Export it from [Frameworks](/docs/frameworks#exports-for-auditors).

**Vendor (third party)**: Any outside company you depend on or share data with. See [Vendors](/docs/vendors).

**Vendor questionnaire**: A standard set of security questions you send to a vendor. See [Vendor questionnaires](/docs/vendor-questionnaires).
